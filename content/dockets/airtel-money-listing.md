## The story so far

Airtel Money is the mobile-money business of Airtel Africa, the London-listed telecoms group that runs Airtel in Kenya and 13 other African countries. It had about 53 million monthly active users and more than 2.3 million agents at 30 June 2026, processed about US$213 billion in the twelve months to that date, and earned about US$1.3 billion in revenue in the year to March 2026 at an EBITDA margin of about 50%. Airtel Africa owns 77.85% of it; TPG, Mastercard, the Qatar Investment Authority and Chimetech bought minority stakes in 2021 for a combined US$550 million.

Airtel Africa first aimed to list Airtel Money in the first half of 2026, and early reporting spoke of raising US$1.5 billion to US$2 billion at a valuation of up to US$10 billion. The float was postponed in May as war in the Middle East unsettled markets, and London was confirmed as the venue in July. On 17 September Bloomberg reported a smaller target of at least US$800 million, and on 23 September Airtel Money published its intention to float on the Main Market of the London Stock Exchange. The offer will consist only of existing shares sold by current shareholders, so the company raises nothing itself; its chief executive, Ian Ferrao, has pointed to a balance sheet with no debt as the reason.

## Why it matters

A separate listing lets investors value the payments business on its own numbers, which a telecoms group's share price tends to blur, and the price it achieves will become a reference point for every other African mobile-money business weighing a sale or a float. At the reported size it would still be the largest IPO in the United Kingdom since 2021, and it confirms that the largest African fintech listings are still heading to London, where Airtel Africa itself listed in 2019.

For Kenya, it will put a public value on a business that competes for the same customers as the country's largest mobile-money service, and it raises the question every payments regulator in Airtel Money's markets will ask of the prospectus: who will own the licensed businesses after the offer, and in what proportions.

## What to watch

The prospectus, expected in early October, will carry the price range and the number of shares on offer, and so the first number Airtel Money and its selling shareholders put on the business. The final price, set by book-building, is expected in mid-October, followed by the first day of trading. Watch which shareholders sell and how much, whether Airtel Africa keeps a clear majority, and how the prospectus deals with regulatory consents in markets such as Kenya, where payments businesses are licensed and supervised by the central bank.
