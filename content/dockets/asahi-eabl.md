## The story so far

In December 2025 Diageo agreed to sell its controlling interest in East African Breweries PLC (EABL) to Asahi Group Holdings. The deal is struck one level above the listed company. Asahi buys all of Diageo Kenya Limited from Diageo Holdings Netherlands B.V., and Diageo Kenya is the vehicle that holds about 65% of EABL; Asahi also takes Diageo's stake in UDV (Kenya), the spirits business. Diageo expects net proceeds of about US$2.3 billion after tax and costs, and the price implies an enterprise value for EABL of about US$4.8 billion, or 17 times adjusted EBITDA.

Because control of a listed company changes hands, the take-over rules in each of the three markets where EABL is listed would ordinarily have required Asahi to offer to buy out the other 35%. Asahi applied to the capital markets regulators in Kenya, Tanzania and Uganda for exemptions and announced on 14 May 2026 that all three had granted them. A minority shareholder had already written to the Capital Markets Authority in April arguing that buying the holding company is still a change of control of EABL, and that every shareholder should be offered the Diageo price. After the exemption was granted, minority shareholders took that argument to the Capital Markets Tribunal.

The courts have moved in parallel. A distributor, Bia Tosha, spent the first half of the year trying to stop the sale over a dispute with EABL that dates back to 2016, and the High Court dismissed its applications in April and again in June. A separate constitutional petition by shareholder Christine Irungu led first to conservatory orders in June and then, on 2 September, to an order by Justice Francis Gikonyo that EABL's ownership and control stay as they were on 18 June 2026 until the tribunal decides the appeal and the Competition Authority of Kenya completes its review. The Competition Authority cleared the deal on 11 September on two conditions. On the terms of the court's order, the tribunal's decision is now the step the deal is waiting for.

## Why it matters

The deal is testing a question that matters to every buyer of a company listed in Nairobi: when control is bought through a holding company that sits above the listed issuer, should the other shareholders be offered the same price? The Capital Markets (Take-overs and Mergers) Regulations, 2002 allow the Capital Markets Authority to exempt a buyer from the mandatory offer, and the Authority used that power here in step with its counterparts in Tanzania and Uganda. The minority shareholders' appeal asks the Capital Markets Tribunal to decide whether the exemption was properly granted, and its answer will shape how future control deals on the Nairobi Securities Exchange are priced and structured.

It also shows how many forums a large transaction now has to pass through: three capital markets regulators, three competition authorities, a tribunal and two separate lines of High Court litigation. Buyers of Kenyan listed companies should expect each of them to be used, and should draft long-stop dates that can absorb a tribunal timetable.

## What to watch

The Capital Markets Tribunal's decision is the next date that matters, and no hearing date has been published. If the tribunal upholds the exemption, both conditions in the status quo order will have been met and the parties can move to completion, unless the petitioner obtains fresh orders and subject to any approvals still outstanding in Tanzania and Uganda, where no decision has been reported. If the tribunal sets the exemption aside, Asahi faces a mandatory offer for the remaining 35% or a further round of litigation. Watch as well for how Diageo Kenya complies with the Competition Authority's conditions on cooler space and on setting aside proceeds for liabilities, and for the hearing of the constitutional petition on its merits.
