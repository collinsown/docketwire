## The story so far

The road from Mau Summit through Eldoret to Malaba carries Kenya's trade with Uganda and the markets beyond it, and it faces heavy congestion and high accident rates. The Government plans to widen its 243 kilometres into a dual carriageway, with safety works, intelligent transport systems and climate-resilient design, and to deliver it as a public private partnership in which a private partner designs, finances, builds, operates and maintains the road.

Preparation is under way. The Multilateral Cooperation Center for Development Finance approved a US$3.15 million grant for a full feasibility study in November 2025, covering engineering, environmental and social safeguards and potential tolling structures. On 3 February 2026 the National Treasury's PPP Directorate, the Kenya National Highways Authority and the Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank started a four-month pre-feasibility study with a joint venture of CPCS of Canada and Avatech Engineering of Kenya. The Treasury's timetable, subject to approvals, is a request for qualifications in the third quarter of 2026, a request for proposals in the first quarter of 2027 and commercial close in the second quarter of 2027.

## Why it matters

The project extends a model the Government has already adopted on the same corridor: the Rironi to Nakuru to Mau Summit highway is being delivered as a public private partnership, reported at about US$1.5 billion. If both proceed, most of the main road from Nairobi to the Uganda border would be financed privately and repaid over time.

For lenders and sponsors, the questions are the ones every Kenyan road concession raises: how traffic and toll revenue risk is shared, what support the Government gives under the Public Private Partnerships Act, 2021, and how construction is managed on a busy road that has to stay open to freight throughout.

## What to watch

The request for qualifications is the first public procurement step, and no notice had been reported by late September, with the quarter ending on 30 September. Watch for the notice itself, the appointment of the feasibility study and transaction adviser, the PPP Committee's approval of the feasibility study, which comes before procurement moves to proposals, and the shortlist of qualified bidders.
