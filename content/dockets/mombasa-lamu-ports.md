## The story so far

Kenya has been working to bring private operators into its two main ports for several years. In September 2023 the Kenya Ports Authority invited firms to prequalify for 25-year public private partnerships covering four assets: Lamu Container Terminal berths 1 to 3, the Lamu Special Economic Zone, berths 11 to 14 at Mombasa and Mombasa's Container Terminal 1. The process was later reworked, with the Treasury choosing to assess and structure the port assets as a portfolio rather than one by one, and new feasibility studies began in October 2025.

On 27 August 2026 the PPP Committee approved those feasibility studies, moving the projects into procurement. The first phase will be three transactions: a concession for Mombasa berths 11 to 14, a concession for Container Terminal 1, and an integrated concession for the Lamu Container Terminal and the Lamu Special Economic Zone. The model is a landlord port, in which the Kenya Ports Authority keeps ownership and strategic oversight while private operators bring capital, equipment and operating expertise. Market engagement was due to start in September 2026.

## Why it matters

These are among the largest public private partnerships Kenya has attempted, with the first phase reported at about US$1 billion, and they go to the heart of the ports' competitiveness against rival harbours in the region. Berths 11 to 14 alone are reported to need about US$300 million of redevelopment, taking annual yard capacity from about 300,000 to about 900,000 containers.

Port concessions are also sensitive for the people who work in them. Shippers have backed the plan, while stakeholders have pressed for transparency and job security, and the Kenya Ports Authority has presented this round as bringing private investment into operations while ownership stays public.

## What to watch

Market engagement with potential investors is the next step, followed by formal procurement of each of the three transactions. Watch for the qualification criteria (the 2023 round required at least ten years of container terminal experience), the concession terms on tariffs and staff, the order in which the three transactions go to market, and the second phase of port assets, which is already in feasibility.
