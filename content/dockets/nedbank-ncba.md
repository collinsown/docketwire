## The story so far

On 21 January 2026 Nedbank Group served notice on NCBA Group's board, the Capital Markets Authority, the Nairobi Securities Exchange and the Competition Authority of Kenya that it intended to acquire about 66% of NCBA. It chose a partial pro rata offer: every NCBA shareholder could tender 66% of their holding, and could apply to sell more, subject to scaling. The price was KSh 105 a share, paid as KSh 21 in cash and 0.0402994 new Nedbank shares for each NCBA share tendered, which valued NCBA at about 1.4 times book value. Holders whose entitlement would come to fewer than 200 Nedbank shares were offered all cash at KSh 105 instead.

Kenyan take-over rules would ordinarily require a buyer of effective control to offer for every share, so the structure needed an exemption. The CMA granted it on 19 February, by which time holders of 77.54% of NCBA had given irrevocable undertakings to accept. The offer ran from 28 May to 10 July and drew acceptances for about 79.9% of NCBA's shares against the 66% Nedbank wanted, so the excess applications will be scaled back.

The approvals came in a long sequence across the markets where the two groups operate. Competition clearances came from COMESA, the East African Community Competition Authority, Tanzania's Fair Competition Commission and the ECOWAS Regional Competition Authority. Banking approvals came from the South African Reserve Bank, the National Bank of Rwanda and the Bank of Tanzania, and on 28 August from the Central Bank of Kenya under section 13(4) of the Banking Act.

## Why it matters

Nedbank's route matters as much as its price. A partial offer lets a buyer take control without buying the whole register, and it gives every shareholder the same chance to sell the same proportion at the same price, which is the protection the mandatory offer rule exists to secure. It also keeps NCBA listed, with about 34% of its shares in public hands and its brand and local management in place, so the Nairobi market keeps one of its largest banks.

For Kenyan banking, the deal brings a large South African group into a sector that is consolidating as the minimum core capital for banks rises in stages to KSh 10 billion by 2029 under the Business Laws (Amendment) Act, 2024. The Central Bank of Kenya said the acquisition would support stability, strengthen the resilience of the banking sector and promote competition.

## What to watch

Completion is the step that remains. Nedbank has kept its indicative timing at the end of the third quarter or early in the fourth quarter of 2026. Accepting shareholders then receive their Nedbank shares and a cash total of about R3.0 billion, roughly KSh 23 billion, and final results follow on the tenth trading day after settlement. After that, watch how Nedbank reconstitutes NCBA's board, whether NCBA's remaining 34% stays liquid on the NSE, and how Kenyan investors who now hold Johannesburg-listed Nedbank shares choose to keep or sell them.
