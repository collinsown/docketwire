## The story so far

Quickmart opened its first store in Nakuru in 2006 and has grown into Kenya's second-largest modern grocery retailer by store count and turnover, with 72 stores in 16 counties and an estimated 15% of the modern grocery market. Its only shareholder is Sokoni Retail Kenya Limited, the vehicle through which funds managed by the private equity firm Adenia, the founders of Quickmart and Tumaini, and the group chief executive hold their interests. For 2025 Quickmart reported revenue of KSh 50.4 billion and adjusted profit after tax of KSh 1.7 billion, with revenue growing at 18.4% a year between 2021 and 2025.

On 23 September 2026 Quick Mart PLC announced its intention to float on the Main Investment Market Segment of the Nairobi Securities Exchange. Sokoni proposes to sell 2 billion existing shares, half of the company, with an over-allotment option of up to 15% more if demand allows; using all of it would leave Sokoni with 42.5%. No new shares are being issued, so Quickmart raises nothing and the proceeds go to Sokoni, which has structured the sale as a pro rata partial exit for its own shareholders. The company has filed its application and a draft information memorandum with the Capital Markets Authority and the NSE, and expects the offer to open on or around 30 September.

## Why it matters

If it proceeds, the offer will be a test of whether the Nairobi Securities Exchange can work as an exit route for the private equity funds that own many of Kenya's mid-sized companies. It follows the Government's sale of 65% of Kenya Pipeline Company at KSh 9 a share in an offer that closed in February 2026, which ended more than a decade without a Nairobi IPO, and it would make Quickmart the second supermarket chain on the exchange after Uchumi.

The structure matters to investors. An offer for sale changes who owns the company without adding to its balance sheet, so the case for buying rests on the business as it stands and on the dividends it pays. Quickmart has said it expects to pay KSh 0.50 a share for 2026, and the price in the information memorandum will fix the yield on offer.

## What to watch

The CMA's approval and the information memorandum, which will carry the price and the timetable, are expected around 30 September. The price sets the value: with 4 billion shares in issue, every shilling of offer price values the whole company at KSh 4 billion. After that, watch the allocation between Kenyan retail and institutional investors, investors elsewhere in the East African Community and foreign investors, the use of the over-allotment option, and the first day of trading on the NSE.
