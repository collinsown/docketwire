## The story so far

Nigeria's Zenith Bank Plc agreed to acquire the whole of Paramount Bank Limited, and the deal passed four gates between January and April 2026. The Competition Authority of Kenya approved it on 22 January on condition that Zenith keep all 78 of Paramount's employees for at least 12 months after completion. The Central Bank of Kenya approved the transaction under the Banking Act on 9 March, and the National Treasury followed on 16 March. The Central Bank of Nigeria, whose approval Zenith also needed, gave its consent before completion, although the date was not published. Zenith completed the purchase of 100% of Paramount in April 2026, giving it a licensed bank in Kenya.

## Why it matters

Buying an existing licensed bank gives a foreign group a working platform in Kenya from the day of completion, and small banks are under pressure to grow or sell as the minimum core capital requirement rises in stages to KSh 10 billion by 2029 under the Business Laws (Amendment) Act, 2024. With 78 employees, Paramount sits at the small end of the sector, and a well-capitalised owner is one answer to that pressure.

The Competition Authority's condition is typical of its approach. It can weigh public interest factors, including employment, when it reviews a merger under the Competition Act, 2010, and job-retention conditions like this one appear often in its decisions.

## What to watch

The docket is closed. What remains to watch is the integration: whether Paramount trades under the Zenith name, how quickly Zenith grows the balance sheet it bought, and how the 12-month employment condition, which runs until about April 2027, is met.
