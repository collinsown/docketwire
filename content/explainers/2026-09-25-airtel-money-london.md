## The business being listed

Airtel Money is the mobile-money arm of Airtel Africa, the London-listed telecoms group that runs Airtel in Kenya and 13 other African countries. It launched in 2011 and now offers person-to-person transfers, international remittances, bill and merchant payments, cash deposits and withdrawals through agents, and access to small loans and savings. At 30 June 2026 it had about 53 million monthly active users and more than 2.3 million agents, and it processed about US$213 billion in the twelve months to that date. In the year to March 2026 it earned about US$1.3 billion in revenue, at an EBITDA margin of about 50%.

Airtel Africa owns 77.85% of the business. TPG, Mastercard, the Qatar Investment Authority and Chimetech bought minority stakes in 2021 for a combined US$550 million, and those investors now have a route to sell. Airtel Africa has said that it expects to remain a long-term strategic shareholder after the float.

## What an offer for sale means

> ### What is an offer for sale?
> In an offer for sale, existing shareholders sell some of their shares to new investors. The company issues no new shares and receives none of the money, so its balance sheet is unchanged. A primary offer is the other route: the company creates new shares and keeps the capital they raise.

Airtel Money's float is an offer for sale. Its chief executive, Ian Ferrao, has linked that choice to the balance sheet: the business has no debt, so it does not need new capital, and the offer consists solely of shares sold by existing shareholders. The float therefore does three things for its owners. It turns part of their holdings into cash, it gives the business a share price that the market sets every day, and it creates a currency that can later be used for employee incentives or acquisitions.

The same structure is being used in Nairobi this month. Quickmart's proposed listing on the Nairobi Securities Exchange (No. 8 of 2026) is also a sale of existing shares by its owner, with no money going to the company.

## Why the target came down

Airtel Africa first aimed to list Airtel Money in the first half of 2026, and reporting early in the year spoke of raising US$1.5 billion to US$2 billion at a valuation of up to US$10 billion. In May the group postponed the float to the second half of the year, citing unfavourable market conditions linked to the war involving Iran, and in July it confirmed London as the preferred venue. On 17 September Bloomberg reported that the target had been cut to at least US$800 million, up to 60% below the earlier range, and the Financial Times reported a valuation target of US$8 billion to US$9 billion.

Two things explain the gap. The first is the market: London has had a long slowdown in new listings, and investors have become more selective about pricing. The second is the structure: in an offer for sale, the size of the deal is whatever the selling shareholders choose to sell at a price the market will pay, so a lower price can simply mean that they sell less now and keep more for later.

## Why London

Airtel Africa itself listed on the London Stock Exchange in 2019, and its reporting currency is the US dollar, so London gives Airtel Money the same investor base that already owns its parent. At the reported size it would still be the largest IPO in the United Kingdom since 2021. In August the company was reported to be considering pricing the shares in US dollars rather than sterling, in line with its parent's reporting currency.

## Who has to say yes

The Financial Conduct Authority must approve the prospectus and admit the shares to listing, and the London Stock Exchange must admit them to trading on its Main Market. Each of Airtel Money's markets also has a central bank that licenses its mobile-money business and decides who may own or control it. In Kenya, payment service providers are licensed and supervised by the Central Bank of Kenya. The prospectus is the document that will set out which of those consents the offer needs and whether they have been obtained.

## What to look for in the prospectus

The prospectus, expected in early October, will carry the price range and the number of shares on offer. Readers should look first at which shareholders are selling and how much, and at the stake Airtel Africa will keep. After that come the lock-up periods, which fix how long the sellers must wait before selling more; the dividend policy; the agreements between Airtel Money and the Airtel telecoms companies for the network, the brand and the agents the two businesses share; and the risk factors on currency movements, taxes on mobile-money transactions and licensing in each market.

## What it means for Kenya

For Kenyan readers, the float will put a public value on a business that competes for the same customers as the country's largest mobile-money service. That value will be used as a reference point when other African payments businesses consider a sale, a partnership or a listing of their own, including on the Nairobi Securities Exchange. The final price is expected in mid-October, and the docket records each step as it happens.
