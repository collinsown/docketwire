## What's going on here?

Airtel Money is the mobile-money arm of Airtel Africa. It serves 56.5 million customers and handles an annualised transaction value above US$245 billion. On 23 July, Airtel Africa confirmed London as the preferred venue for listing the business in 2026, having postponed the float in May when the conflict in the Middle East unsettled markets and pushed up energy costs.

On 17 September, Bloomberg reported that the group is weighing a raise of at least US$800 million, as much as 60% below the US$1.5 billion to US$2 billion discussed in April.

## Why carve out mobile money?

> ### What is a carve-out?
> A carve-out separates one business from a group and gives it its own shares, board and accounts, so that investors can buy it on its own. It is used when a division is worth more valued separately than as a line inside its parent.

Payments businesses are valued like technology companies; mobile networks are valued like utilities. Separating them lets each be priced on its own terms and gives the payments arm its own currency for acquisitions. The cost is a group reorganisation across every country where the business operates, with licences, contracts and staff moving to new entities.

## Why London, and why smaller?

London remains the deepest market for African-listed companies, and Airtel Africa is already listed there. A smaller raise keeps the listing alive while investors decide how to price a regulated African payments business against rising interest rates. The size of the raise can be settled close to launch, once demand is clearer.

## Who has to say yes?

- The central bank in each market. Mobile-money operators are licensed payment service providers, and a change in who controls them typically needs the regulator's consent. In Kenya that is the Central Bank of Kenya.
- The UK listing authority and the exchange, on the prospectus and the admission.
- Data protection regulators, where customer data moves between entities or across borders. Kenya's Office of the Data Protection Commissioner sits in that group.

## What does it mean for Kenya?

Airtel Money Kenya competes with M-Pesa, which now sits inside a Safaricom that Vodacom has controlled since its 30 June purchase of a further stake. Two mobile-money businesses, both answering to shareholders abroad, will each be watching how the other is regulated, priced and taxed.

## What would junior lawyers do?

On the issuer side, verification of every statement in the prospectus, due diligence across each market, the group reorganisation documents and a consent tracker for every central bank. On the regulator side, the licence conditions that follow a change of control. On the competitor side, the reading of the prospectus for what it discloses about the market.
