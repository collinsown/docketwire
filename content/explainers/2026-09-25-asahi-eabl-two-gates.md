## The transaction

Diageo announced on 17 December 2025 that it had agreed to sell its controlling interest in East African Breweries PLC (EABL) to Asahi Group Holdings. The sale is structured one level above the listed company. Asahi will buy the whole of Diageo Kenya Limited from Diageo Holdings Netherlands B.V., and Diageo Kenya is the company that holds about 65% of EABL. Asahi will also take Diageo's stake in UDV (Kenya), the spirits business.

Diageo expects net proceeds of about US$2.3 billion after tax and transaction costs. The price implies an enterprise value for EABL of about US$4.8 billion, or 17 times adjusted EBITDA, and reporting at the time of the minority objection put the implied price at about KSh 590 for each EABL share. For Diageo, the sale is part of a plan under its chief executive, Sir Dave Lewis, to reduce debt. For Asahi, it brings a brewer with operations in Kenya, Uganda and Tanzania and net sales of KSh 128.8 billion in the year to June 2025. Asahi has said that it intends to keep EABL listed and to hold 65%, with no plan to buy the shares held by the public.

## Why the take-over rules are engaged

> ### What is a mandatory offer?
> When a person acquires effective control of a company listed in Nairobi, the Capital Markets (Take-overs and Mergers) Regulations, 2002 generally require that person to offer to buy the shares of every other shareholder. The rule exists so that the minority can leave on fair terms when control of their company changes hands. The Capital Markets Authority can exempt a buyer from the obligation.

Asahi's purchase is of a private company, Diageo Kenya Limited, whose main asset is a controlling stake in a listed one. Asahi itself acknowledged that the purchase would give it indirect control of 65% of EABL and would ordinarily bring the take-over requirements into play. That is why it applied for exemptions, and why it applied in all three countries where EABL is listed: on the Nairobi Securities Exchange, and through cross-listings on the Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange and the Uganda Securities Exchange.

## The exemption and the objection

Asahi applied to the Capital Markets Authority in Kenya, the Capital Markets and Securities Authority in Tanzania and the Capital Markets Authority of Uganda, and announced on 14 May 2026 that each had granted an exemption. The effect is that Asahi takes control of EABL without offering to buy the remaining 35%.

The objection was on the record before the decision. On 1 April, lawyers for shareholder Shane Ngechu wrote to the Capital Markets Authority arguing that an exemption would undermine the take-over rules. The acquisition was structured at holding company level, they said, but its effect was to give Asahi indirect control of a listed company without an offer to all shareholders, and the Authority should require a full offer on terms similar to those agreed with Diageo. After the exemption was granted, minority shareholders appealed to the Capital Markets Tribunal, where the appeal is pending.

The case for the exemption is also clear. EABL stays listed with the same public float, the minority keep their shares and the market in them, one foreign controlling shareholder is replaced by another, and a full offer would add the cost of the remaining 35% to a price that was negotiated for 65%. Regulators in three countries reached the same conclusion on the same application.

## Two lines of litigation

The deal has also drawn two separate sets of court proceedings.

The first came from Bia Tosha Distributors, which has been in a dispute with EABL over distribution since 2016 and values its claim at about KSh 25 billion. In January it asked the High Court to stop the sale, arguing that if Diageo left Kenya it would be harder to enforce any judgment in its favour. By consent, interim orders restrained only the final steps of completion while regulatory work went on. On 9 April Justice Bahati Mwamuye dismissed the application and lifted the orders; the court noted that a commercial dispute between private parties does not by itself justify suspending a major transaction, and that remedies would remain open to the distributor if it succeeded. In June the court dismissed a second application, because Bia Tosha had already sought similar relief in the Court of Appeal.

The second is a constitutional petition by shareholder Christine Irungu, first filed in Machakos. It alleges failures of disclosure and of minority protection by Diageo, EABL and the regulators, and it questions whether Diageo's 2022 and 2023 tender offer, which took its stake from about 50% to about 65%, was a step towards the sale. Conservatory orders froze EABL's ownership as it stood on 18 June 2026. Diageo and Asahi argued that the dispute belonged before the specialist bodies, the Capital Markets Tribunal and the Competition Tribunal, and that the court should not take over their functions. On 2 September Justice Francis Gikonyo held that the dispute raised constitutional questions that no single regulator could resolve, and ordered that ownership and control stay as they were on 18 June until the tribunal decides the appeal and the Competition Authority of Kenya completes its review.

## What the Competition Authority decided

The Competition Authority of Kenya approved the deal on 11 September 2026. It cleared UDV (Kenya) without conditions and Diageo Kenya on two. The first governs conduct after completion: at least 20% of the cooler space the business supplies to retail outlets must stay open to rival beer and cider brands, with carve-outs for supermarkets, fuel-station liquor stores, upmarket bars and hotels above two stars. The second is financial: part of the sale proceeds must be set aside to settle outstanding liabilities before the deal completes.

The cooler condition reflects how beer is sold in East Africa. Brewers lend refrigerators to bars and shops, and a cooler filled with one brewer's brands is shelf space its rivals cannot reach. Keeping a fifth of that space open costs the buyer far less than selling a brand or a brewery, and the Authority will need a way to monitor it outlet by outlet.

## What the tribunal has to decide

The tribunal's question is narrower than the petition's. It will be asked whether the Capital Markets Authority used its power to exempt properly, and how much weight the minority's interests should carry when control changes hands through a holding company. The minority will say that an indirect acquisition should be treated in the same way as a direct one, and that the one protection the rules give a minority on a change of control is the chance to sell at the control price. Asahi and the Authority will say that the exemption power exists for cases like this one, where the company stays listed, the free float is unchanged and the new controller takes the old controller's place without buying any shares from the public.

The ruling will be read by every adviser structuring a control deal in Nairobi. If the tribunal upholds the exemption, it confirms that an indirect change of control can be cleared without an offer where the listing survives. If it sets the exemption aside, buyers will be pushed towards partial or full offers, and towards pricing the minority into the deal from the start.

## What it means for deal-making in Nairobi

Three lessons stand out for buyers and their advisers.

Firstly, the take-over exemption is now a contested step. A buyer that needs one should expect a challenge, build the tribunal's timetable into its long-stop date, and prepare its disclosure on the footing that it will be tested in open court.

Secondly, the number of forums has grown. EABL has needed three capital markets regulators, three competition authorities, a tribunal and two lines of High Court litigation, and each can hold a deal at the last step after the others have cleared it.

Finally, the price of control has become a public question. Nedbank's partial offer for NCBA (No. 3 of 2026) gave every shareholder the same chance to sell the same proportion at the same price, and the Asahi appeal asks whether that should be the expectation whenever control of a listed Kenyan company changes hands.

## What happens next

The tribunal has not published a hearing date, and competition decisions in Tanzania and Uganda have not been reported. Diageo expected completion in the second half of 2026. The docket records each step as it happens.
