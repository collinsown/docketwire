Docket Wire keeps a live file on each deal and dispute that reaches a regulator or a court in Kenya and East Africa. Each file, a docket, shows who has to say yes, what has happened so far and what comes next. Every step links to its source.

## How a matter becomes a docket

A docket opens when a primary source confirms a deal or a dispute: a company announcement, a filing, a regulator's decision or a court order. It takes a number in the order the Wire opened it, so No. 3 of 2026 is the third docket opened in 2026.

Each docket has three parts. The gates are the approvals and challenges the matter has to pass: competition and capital markets regulators, banking regulators, tribunals and courts. The docket sheet records every step in date order. Next holds the dates coming up, which also feed the cause list and its calendar.

A gate appears only once a source reports it, and a docket closes when the deal completes, is withdrawn or is blocked.

## Explainers

Some matters get a longer read: what happened, who had to say yes and what the lawyers did. Each explainer sits on its docket.

## Independence

Docket Wire is an independent publication. It is not written for or on behalf of any law firm or client, and it draws only on public sources. Nothing on this site is legal advice.
