Docket Wire is an independent record of the deals and disputes that have to pass East Africa's regulators, tribunals and courts before they can close. Each matter gets its own file, a docket, which sets out the story so far, every approval and challenge on the record, each step in date order with its source, and the dates coming up. Longer analysis explains what the matters mean for the people who structure, finance and advise on them.

## What we cover

We follow acquisitions of listed and unlisted companies, bank consolidations, listings and public offers, and the public private partnerships that are moving Kenya's roads and ports into private finance. The gates on a docket are the authorities whose decision decides whether a deal closes: competition regulators such as the Competition Authority of Kenya, COMESA and the East African Community Competition Authority; capital markets regulators and exchanges; the Central Bank of Kenya and the National Treasury on banking deals; the PPP Committee on concessions; and the tribunals and courts that hear challenges to all of them. Each authority has its own page listing the matters before it and what it has decided.

## How a matter becomes a docket

A docket opens when a primary source confirms a deal or a dispute: a company announcement, a filing, a regulator's decision or a court order. It takes a number in the order the Wire opened it, so No. 3 of 2026 is the third docket opened in 2026. A docket closes when the deal completes, is withdrawn or is blocked, and its file stays online.

## How to read a docket

The gate track at the top of each docket shows every approval and challenge in sequence, ending in completion. A filled node has been cleared, a half-filled node has been cleared on conditions, an open node is pending, and a red node marks a challenge or a court order holding the deal. Below the track, the story so far explains the transaction in plain language, followed by why it matters and what to watch. The gates section records each authority's decision as reported, the docket sheet lists every step in date order, and the next section feeds the cause list and its calendar.

## Sources and corrections

Every entry on a docket links to its source, and we prefer primary sources: announcements, regulatory filings, decisions and orders. Where a fact rests on press reporting, the entry says so through its link. If a source is wrong or a docket is out of date, tell us and we will correct the record and note the change.

## Independence

Docket Wire is an independent publication. It is not written for or on behalf of any law firm, client or party to the matters it covers, and it draws only on public sources. Nothing on this site is legal, financial or investment advice.
